Getting started

1. Install the virtual camera

Silhouette must be in your Applications folder. On first launch it asks macOS to install its camera extension; approve it once in System Settings › General › Login Items & Extensions › Camera Extensions. The System Settings… button in the sidebar opens that pane. When the sidebar shows Connected at the top, "Silhouette" is available as a camera in other apps; click it for details.

2. Choose a source

The Source tile above the preview shows the current camera or movie, named in the title bar above it. Click the tile to open a picker with a thumbnail of each choice: your cameras, a built-in Test pattern, a bundled Sample video of a green screen shoot, and your recent movies. The + beside Videos adds a movie file as the camera. Right-click a recent movie to remove it. Drop a movie on the preview to use it. The last source is remembered.

3. Set the key colour

Auto runs when you choose a source and sets the key from the frame edges. Click Auto beside Color Range to run it again, or click the colour swatch and then the backdrop in the preview; the preview shows the unkeyed image while you pick. Press Esc to cancel. Then tighten Color Range until the backdrop is gone and the subject is intact.

4. Pick a background

Click the Background tile above the preview to open its picker. None leaves the backdrop transparent (black in most video apps, a checkerboard in the preview). Recent images and movies, web pages, and colours each have a section, with + to add a new one. See Backgrounds.

With a live camera, the preview is mirrored like a mirror, layers included, so text in a background reads backwards there. The virtual camera output is never mirrored, so the people on the call see it the right way round. Many call apps also mirror your own self-view. View › Mirror Preview turns the preview mirroring off.

5. Close the window

Closing the window keeps Silhouette running in the menu bar, so the virtual camera keeps working. While no app is using the camera, Silhouette pauses capture to save power, and it starts again within about a second when an app opens the camera. Use the menu bar item to open the window again or to quit.
