Controls

Every slider shows its value on the right. Click the value to type a number, or step it with ↑ and ↓ (⇧ for ten times the step). Press ⌘K to hide or show the sidebar.

Camera

4K keyingCaptures at 3840×2160 when the camera supports it, keys at that size, and downsamples to the 1080p output. Finer hair and edges with a 4K camera, about four times the GPU work.
PauseThe pause button in the Source header freezes the current frame so you can tune the key and shadow without movement. The virtual camera keeps showing the frozen frame until you press play. Changing the source goes back to live video.
MirrorMirrors the preview of a live camera like a mirror. Movies, the sample video, and the test pattern always show as recorded. The virtual camera output is never mirrored.

The frames-per-second and GPU-time readout sits at the top right of the window, above the preview.

Chromakey

Pixel values here are measured on the 1920×1080 output, so a setting means the same thing with a 720p, 1080p, or 4K source.

Key colour and matte controls share one group. Click the triangle in its header to show or hide the sliders; the colour swatch, Auto, and the on/off switch stay visible. With the switch off the camera passes through untouched, and the key and Shadow controls dim because they have nothing to act on.

Green screen / Person / AppleWhere the matte comes from. Green screen keys a real backdrop by colour, with all the controls below. Person finds you with on-device segmentation and needs no backdrop: the colour tools disappear, Shrink, Blur, and Stabilize still apply, and the readout at the top right shows the time the mask takes each frame. Edges are softer than a lit green screen. Apple uses the Mac's own Background effect as a virtual green screen: click Save Green Image…, then in Video Effects… turn on Background and choose that image as a custom background. Silhouette keys the green as soon as it appears. This gives the best edges without a backdrop, and works with the built-in camera and iPhone Continuity Camera on macOS 15 or later.
ColourThe backdrop colour. Click the swatch to see the unkeyed image, then click the backdrop in the preview. Auto watches the frame for about a second, finds the dominant colour around the edges while skipping the face and the body below it, and sets Color Range so nearly all of that edge is removed and the face is left alone. It also measures how far the backdrop bleeds into the subject's edge and sets Shrink and Blur to match, and sets Desaturate from the key-coloured tint it finds on the subject. Stabilize is left alone. Auto runs by itself the first time you choose a source; after that each source remembers its own colour and range.
Color RangeTwo values, in percent of the full chroma span. Left: how close a colour must be to the key to be fully removed. Right: where the transition to opaque ends. Typical keys sit between 5 and 20. The slider runs to 30; typing or arrowing in the fields can go further. The bar shows the colours along that axis, from the key through neutral toward its complement, and the thumbs take the colour they sit on.
ShrinkPulls the matte edge in by this many pixels to trim fringes.
BlurSoftens the matte edge, in pixels.
DesaturateRemoves the backdrop's tint reflected onto the subject, in percent.
StabilizeBlends the matte with the previous frame where the picture is static, which stops edge flicker from sensor noise. Percent of the previous frame kept; movement passes straight through.

Shadow

The switch in the header turns the shadow on or off. The controls stay in place, dimmed, while it is off.

OpacityStrength of the drop shadow behind the subject, in percent.
SizeHow far the shadow spreads, in output pixels. Larger is softer and fainter at the edge.
OffsetDrag the dot to position the shadow; double-click to reset. The value is in percent of the frame, x then y.
Vary with distanceScales the shadow with the tracked face: nearer gives a longer, softer, lighter shadow; farther a shorter, sharper, denser one. See Face tracking and the shadow.

Face tracking

The switch in the header runs face tracking on the Neural Engine. Web pages receive pose, eye, distance and landmark data, see Web page API, and the shadow's Vary with distance uses the face size. Turning on Vary with distance turns tracking on.

Show on preview draws what the tracker knows over the preview, never in the output: landmark contours and pupils. Pose, distance, and the pose matrix go to web pages through the Web page API; the distance assumes a 63 mm eye separation and a 70° field of view, so it is a relative figure.

High frame rate tracks every frame, 30 times a second, with a face detection every other frame. Off, it tracks every second frame with a detection every fourth. On costs about twice the Neural Engine work and gives smoother pose and eye data.
