Controls

Every slider shows its value on the right. Click the value to type a number, or step it with ↑ and ↓ (⇧ for ten times the step). Press ⌘K to hide or show the sidebar.

Presets

The pop-up at the top of the sidebar shows the current preset: a saved scene with the source, both layers, the key, lighting, shadow, and tracking settings. It is always the one you are editing, and every change is kept in it as you make it; there is nothing to save. Choose another preset to switch to it. The menu also offers New Preset…, which names a new preset made from the current settings and selects it, Rename Preset…, and Delete…, which asks before removing the current preset. The Presets menu in the menu bar lists them with ⌘1 to ⌘9 for quick switching, plus Duplicate. On first launch your existing settings become a preset called Default; deleting the last preset starts a new Default from the current settings.

Source

The Source tile above the preview has a resolution button at its bottom right that reads HD or 4K; Mirror and Pause sit on the preview's bottom-right corner, because they act on the preview. A button is blue while it is on. The source tile, the virtual camera output, and every layer are always shown the right way round; only the preview mirrors. The frame rate and GPU time show in a badge at the preview's top right.

HD / 4KHD keys at 1080p. 4K captures at 3840×2160 when the camera supports it, keys at that size, and downsamples to the 1080p output. Finer hair and edges with a 4K camera, about four times the GPU work.
PauseThe pause button on the Source tile freezes the current frame so you can tune the key and shadow without movement. The virtual camera keeps showing the frozen frame until you press play. Changing the source goes back to live video.
MirrorMirrors the preview like a mirror, for any source. The button sits on the preview's bottom-right corner. The sources, the layers, and the virtual camera output are never mirrored.

Chromakey

Pixel values here are measured on the 1920×1080 output, so a setting means the same thing with a 720p, 1080p, or 4K source.

Key colour and matte controls share one group. Its title is a menu that sets where the matte comes from. With the switch in its header off, the camera passes through untouched, and the group and the Shadow controls fold away because they have nothing to act on.

Chromakey menuWhere the matte comes from. Chromakey keys a real backdrop by colour, with all the controls below. Background Removal finds you with on-device segmentation and needs no backdrop: the colour tools disappear, Shrink, Blur, and Stabilize still apply, and the readout at the top right shows the time the mask takes each frame. Edges are softer than a lit green screen. Apple Chromakey… sets up the Mac's own Background effect as a virtual green screen. Its dialog explains the steps and has buttons to save a plain green image and to open Video Effects, where you turn on Background and choose that image. Afterwards the group is in Chromakey mode, and Auto runs as soon as the green appears. This gives the best edges without a backdrop, and works with the built-in camera and iPhone Continuity Camera.
ColourThe backdrop colour, in the swatch to the left of Color Range. Click the swatch to see the unkeyed image, then click the backdrop in the preview. Auto, the small button to the right of Color Range, watches the frame for about a second, finds the dominant colour around the edges while skipping the face and the body below it, and sets Color Range so nearly all of that edge is removed and the face is left alone. It also measures how far the backdrop bleeds into the subject's edge and sets Shrink and Blur to match, and sets Desaturate from the key-coloured tint it finds on the subject. Stabilize is left alone. Auto runs by itself the first time you choose a source; after that each source remembers its own colour and range.
Color RangeTwo values, in percent of the full chroma span. Left: how close a colour must be to the key to be fully removed. Right: where the transition to opaque ends. Typical keys sit between 5 and 20, and the slider runs to 30. Hold the pointer over the bar to see both values. The bar shows the colours along that axis, from the key through neutral toward its complement, and the thumbs take the colour they sit on.
ShrinkPulls the matte edge in by this many pixels to trim fringes.
BlurSoftens the matte edge, in pixels.
DesaturateRemoves the backdrop's tint reflected onto the subject, in percent.
StabilizeBlends the matte with the previous frame where the picture is static, which stops edge flicker from sensor noise. Percent of the previous frame kept; movement passes straight through.

Lighting

Corrections applied to your image only, after keying, so you sit in the background rather than on it. Layers are untouched. The switch in the header turns the whole section off.

TemperatureCooler to the left, warmer to the right, in a range of −100 to 100. Brightness stays the same. Use it when the background is a sunset and you are lit by a cool window, or the other way round.
RelightLets the background light you. Its brightest area on each side acts as a light, so a background that is brighter on the left lights your left side. Your head is lit as a sphere, and with face tracking on, turning toward a light lights that side of your face; the rest of your silhouette is lit as a body, rounded from edge to edge. The background's colour also wraps around your edges. The light multiplies your image, so it shows on light, lit surfaces and leaves dark clothing alone. Needs a background layer.
HighlightsHow much the relight brightens the lit areas as well as tinting them, from 0 (brightness unchanged) to 100 (strong highlights).
AutoMatches the background: measures the background layer's average colour and yours a few times a second and eases your temperature toward the background's. The slider shows the value it chose. Turn it off to keep that value and adjust by hand. With no background layer it settles at zero.

Shadow

The switch in the header turns the shadow on or off. The controls stay in place, dimmed, while it is off.

OpacityStrength of the drop shadow behind the subject, in percent.
SizeHow far the shadow spreads, in output pixels. Larger is softer and fainter at the edge.
OffsetDrag the dot to position the shadow; double-click to reset. The value is in percent of the frame, x then y.
Vary with distanceScales the shadow with the tracked face: nearer gives a longer, softer, lighter shadow; farther a shorter, sharper, denser one. See Face tracking and the shadow.

Face tracking

The switch in the header runs face tracking on the Neural Engine. Web pages receive pose, eye, distance and landmark data, see Web page API, and the shadow's Vary with distance uses the face size. Turning on Vary with distance turns tracking on.

Show on preview draws what the tracker knows over the preview, never in the output: landmark contours and pupils. Pose, distance, and the pose matrix go to web pages through the Web page API; the distance assumes a 63 mm eye separation and a 70° field of view, so it is a relative figure.

High frame rate tracks every frame, 30 times a second, with a face detection every other frame. Off, it tracks every second frame with a detection every fourth. On costs about twice the Neural Engine work and gives smoother pose and eye data.
