Backgrounds

Above the preview, the Background tile shows what is behind you and the Foreground tile shows what is in front; an empty layer shows a plus. The title bar above each tile names the current choice, and a click on the tile opens a picker with a 16:9 thumbnail of every choice: None, the bundled Test page, and your recent images and movies, web pages, and colours. The two layers share the recent lists. Each section's + adds a new entry: a file from your Mac, a web address typed and confirmed with Return, or a colour typed as hex or picked from the colour well. Right-click a recent entry to remove it. Web pages show a globe; files show their name, and web pages show their address without https://www..

None

The keyed-out area is transparent. Most video apps show it as black; the preview shows a checkerboard.

Files

An image or a movie. Images load once; movies loop and decode in hardware. Files you pick are remembered and reopen on the next launch, and recent files reopen from the picker. You can also drop a file on the preview.

Colours

Type a hex colour, with or without the hash, in three, four, six, or eight digits. The last pair of an eight-digit colour is alpha, so #00000080 as a foreground is a half-strength darkening wash.

Foreground

The foreground is composited over the keyed camera and the background, so it needs transparency to be useful: a PNG with an alpha channel, a web page with a transparent body, or a colour with alpha. An opaque image or movie covers the whole frame. Web pages have no default white background in either layer, so a page covers only what it draws; the bundled test page is transparent and works as a foreground.

The buttons at the Foreground tile's bottom right set how it is drawn. Mask, the person symbol, confines the foreground layer to your silhouette: a colour with alpha, such as #FF40C040, becomes a tint on you alone, and a web page with a transparent background can draw light or graphics that stop at your edges. Off, the foreground covers the whole frame. Blend, the overlapping circles, opens a menu that sets how the foreground combines with the picture, with the same formulas as an image editor: Normal lays it on top; Multiply, Screen, Overlay, Soft Light, Hard Light, Color Dodge, Color Burn, Add, Lighten, Darken, Difference, and Exclusion mix it in. With Mask on, the blend applies to you alone: the background behind your edges stays as it is.

Scene packs

Some backgrounds come as a pair: the scene, and a soft colour map named with -foreground, such as Rainy cafe.mp4 and Rainy cafe-foreground.mp4. The map is meant to be multiplied onto you so your light matches the scene. Choose the scene as the background and Silhouette loads the map as the foreground with Mask on and Blend set to Multiply. The app can only read files you have chosen, so select both files in the Open dialog the first time; after that, picking the scene from the recents brings the map with it. Use the MP4 files: WebM copies do not play.

Web pages

A web page rendered at 1920×1080 behind you. Pages can react to face tracking; see Web page API.

Pages are snapshotted about 15 times a second while they change, so they cost more than an image. Static pages back off to 3 snapshots a second.

Fit

The output is always 1920×1080 at 30 frames per second. The camera and the background are scaled to fill it.
